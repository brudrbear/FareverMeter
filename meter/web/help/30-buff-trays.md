# The buff trays

> Four independent trays of the buffs you actually care about, per character.

The game already shows your buffs. The trays exist because it shows **all** of
them, in one strip, at one size — and the two you need to watch mid-fight are in
there somewhere with the food buff and the rested bonus.

A tray is a small window holding only the statuses you put in it. You get
**four**, and they are configured one at a time on the **Buffs** tab: pick a
tray with the numbered buttons at the top, and everything below applies to that
one. The number in brackets is how many buffs it is already watching.

## They belong to your character, not your install

Trays are saved per character. The line under the tray buttons tells you whose
you are editing, and how many other characters have sets saved.

That matters because a healer's tray and a tank's tray have nothing in common,
and the moment they differ, a shared set is one you will change on the wrong
character. If the meter has not yet heard which character you are, it shows the
trays this install last used.

## Adding buffs

**Add a buff** lists every status the game defines — not merely the ones you
have had. The whole list comes out of the game's own data, so you can set a tray
up for a fight before you have ever seen the buff.

Statuses you *have* had this session sort to the top and are marked, which
covers the common case: something just landed on you and you want to know what
it was.

## What each icon can draw

Under **Look**:

* **Icon size** and **Direction** — across or down. A tray you have put down the
  side of the screen wants Down.
* **When not up** — a dim placeholder, or hidden entirely. The placeholder is
  the better default: a tray that changes shape as buffs come and go is one you
  have to re-read every time.

Under the flag switches:

* **Stack count** and **Time remaining** — the two numbers.
* **Clock sweep** — the icon empties like a cooldown as the buff runs out.
* **Flash when it lands** and **Pulse before it drops** — the two moments worth
  catching out of the corner of your eye.

## Where the numbers go

**Numbers on the icon** places the timer and the stack count independently, in
any of nine positions, each with its own size. A big centred timer with a small
corner stack count is a perfectly reasonable thing to want.

Putting both in the same corner is allowed — you might have one of them switched
off — but the panel warns you, because otherwise they draw on top of each other
and the result reads as a rendering fault.

## Moving them

A tray is **Unlocked** by default and can be dragged wherever you like. Lock it
once it is where you want it, so it cannot be nudged mid-fight.

Arriving at the Buffs tab makes every configured tray point at itself, so
"which one is which, and where did I put them" is answered without clicking
through all four.
